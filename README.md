# My Final Project Ideas
I have narrowed my final project ideas to a few different options, including:
## 1. Stamp collection

  Last year, I purchased a bulk selection of used U.S. stamps to use for scrapbooking and journaling. These stamps feature lovely artworks and are largely unorganized. I think it would be an interesting project to digitize these stamps and use a website such as Free Stamp Catalogue (https://www.freestampcatalogue.com/stamps/country/united-states-of-america) to identify the year and artist that correlates to each stamp. This would allow me to get a larger overview of my collection, provide me with knowledge of the dates associated with each stamp, and show how stamp art has changed over the decades.
  
## 2. Antique postcard collection

   I own various antique postcards (mostly from the late 19th/early 20th centuries) that I have purchased at antique stores across the state. These postcards each have unique art and images as well as handwritten messages. This project would work to capture the images of each postcard, include accurate metadata, and transcribe handwritten messages when possible. Since most of these postcards are older than 1930, and thus in the public domain, I would avoid any potential copyright issues.

## 3. Collection of family photographs

  This idea was discouraged in class, so I likely will not utilize it. However, I think it is still worth mentioning. I have always thought that digital libraries and digital humanities are a great way for people to preserve family history and personal items. So many people have family heirlooms and memorabilia that are infused with stories and importance, even if that importance is not understood by those outside of their family. This project would involve digitizing my family photograph collections, several of which are from the early 20th century, and attaching metadata. This metadata would be organized via spreadsheet before being transferred to the CollectionBuilder.
